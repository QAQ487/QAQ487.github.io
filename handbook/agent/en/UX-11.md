# UX-11 · Diegetic UI / In-world interface

**Aliases:** Diegetic vitals; Spine health bar; On-weapon ammo counter; 世界內狀態顯示; ダイジェティックな体力表示

**Group:** UI & HUD
**Subcategory:** Where the Interface Lives

## What you see

A character checks a watch for remaining resources or operates a wall terminal. The information belongs to the story world, rather than existing only for the player. Status can also live on the character: a row of lights along the suit spine is the health bar, a small display on the gun shows remaining ammo, and when hurt the character limps and breathes heavily instead of a corner health bar.

## Not to be confused with

Does looking three-dimensional make something diegetic? No: ask whether its existence is acknowledged within the character’s world. In-world interfaces can also be hard to read or slow to use; they are not inherently better. A red low-health vignette (FB-16) is a screen overlay, not an in-world interface.

## Use it for

A character checks a watch for remaining resources or operates a wall terminal. The information belongs to the story world, rather than existing only for the player. Status can also live on the character: a row of lights along the suit spine is the health bar, a small display on the gun shows remaining ammo, and when hurt the character limps and breathes heavily instead of a corner health bar.

## Tunable parameters

world ownership · character access · physical placement · legibility · interaction distance · fallback · diegetic vitals carriers (suit lights, weapon display, limp / breathing)

## Request prompt

Design an in-world oxygen watch showing 0–100% and 12:30 remaining, readable after a 0.4-second raise-hand action; add a high-contrast mode for bright light and have the watch itself warn below 15%. Also build in-world vitals: five light segments on the suit spine show health and a small screen on the gun shows ammo; below 30% health switch to a limping walk with heavier breathing.

## Implementation hint

Anchor the interface to an object acknowledged by the fictional world and let character actions operate it. Preserve readability across viewing distances and provide a fallback when perspective, lighting, or occlusion makes the in-world surface unusable. For diegetic vitals, drive emissive segments on the suit or weapon mesh from the same health / ammo values the HUD would use, and layer injured locomotion and breathing audio from health thresholds; keep a fallback readout for accessibility.
