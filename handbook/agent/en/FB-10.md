# FB-10 · Slow motion

**Aliases:** Bullet time; Time dilation; Slow-mo; Selective bullet time; Time dilation focus; 選擇性子彈時間; 専心スロー

**Group:** Combat feedback & game feel
**Subcategory:** Time manipulation

## What you see

Game time slows down globally (e.g. to 30%), so characters, particles and projectiles all move slowly; sometimes the player stays faster, and the screen edges darken or desaturate. Selective bullet time (focus aiming): enemies, bullets and debris slow right down while the player's reticle and camera turn stay near full speed, so you can aim calmly in a slowed world; audio goes low-passed (muffled, slightly lower pitch) with a vignette, and sound and speed come back together at the end.

## Not to be confused with

Not a freeze or hitstop (fully stopped) nor a freeze-frame finisher (FB-11). Slow-mo still moves, just slower, and can last seconds. Not stasis with stored impact (FB-31 — only one target is frozen and hits accumulate).

## Use it for

Abilities (bullet time), perfect-dodge rewards, mid-air or behind-cover focus aiming, drama when the last enemy falls.

## Tunable parameters

time scale 0.1–0.5 · ramp in/out · duration · player exemption factor · selective mode: reticle / camera speed near 1× · post effect (desaturate, vignette) · audio pitch & low-pass cutoff

## Request prompt

In your game, trigger 1.5 s of slow motion after a perfect dodge: drop global timeScale to 0.3 within 0.1 s and ramp back to 1 over the final 0.3 s; meanwhile desaturate 40% with a pale-blue vignette, and keep the hero animating at 0.6×. Optional selective bullet time: while the focus button is held, drop the world to 0.2–0.3× but keep the player's reticle and camera near full speed, low-pass the audio, and restore within 0.2 s on release or when the meter runs out.

## Implementation hint

Two clocks: gameDelta = realDelta * timeScale; UI and post-process use realDelta. Post: fullscreen pass with saturation lerp and vignette in a pixel shader. Particles must use gameDelta to slow correctly. Selective bullet time: give the player aim, camera rotation and input a per-entity time scale near 1 while world entities use the slowed clock; drive a low-pass filter cutoff and pitch on the world audio bus from the same blend value.
