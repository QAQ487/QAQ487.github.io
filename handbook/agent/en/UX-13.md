# UX-13 · Spatial UI

**Group:** UI & HUD
**Subcategory:** Where the Interface Lives

## What you see

Nameplates above characters, navigation light trails along the ground, or objective text projected onto a wall for the player: the information is linked to spatial locations.

## Not to be confused with

“Spatial” in this four-part classification does not mean the engine must render it as a 3D mesh. A nameplate projected onto the screen can still have a spatial relationship. Classify its function/narrative status separately from its implementation.

## Use it for

Nameplates above characters, navigation light trails along the ground, or objective text projected onto a wall for the player: the information is linked to spatial locations.

## Tunable parameters

world anchor · screen projection · distance scaling · occlusion · edge clamping · narrative status

## Request prompt

Design nameplates for 4 teammates plus a ground navigation trail: scale nameplates from 5–30 m, hide them behind walls, and retain only the leader arrow off-screen; keep them spatially anchored without treating them as physical world objects.

## Implementation hint

Attach each cue to a world position, then choose world rendering or screen projection independently of its narrative classification. Define distance, occlusion, overlap, and off-screen behavior so spatial relationships remain understandable.
