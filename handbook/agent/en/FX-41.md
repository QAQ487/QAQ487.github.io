# FX-41 · Diegetic-to-score music transition

**Aliases:** Diegetic to non-diegetic transition; Source music to score; Source-to-score crossfade; In-world music becomes the soundtrack; Diegetic music bleed; 場景音樂轉配樂; 畫內音樂轉配樂; 劇中音樂轉場; 劇中音楽から劇伴へ; ソース音楽から劇伴

**Group:** Audio & haptics
**Subcategory:** Audio

## What you see

A song starts from a real object in the scene — a record player, radio, piano or a singer. It has a clear position: turning the camera shifts it between left and right, it carries the room's echo, and walking away makes it quieter and duller. Once the player interacts with it or sits down to rest, the song opens up over a few seconds: it is no longer pinned to the object but spreads evenly across both channels, the echo clears and the sound becomes full and crisp, like proper score, while ambient wind, machinery and the like duck and turn muffled. The song itself never cuts or restarts. When the player leaves, stops the record or a fight begins, it runs in reverse: the music shrinks back into the object and the ambience returns.

## Not to be confused with

Not distance attenuation (FX-23) or air absorption (FX-25) on their own; they are only part of the starting state. Not vertical layering or horizontal resequencing (FX-29, FX-30, which switch stems or sections inside the score); here the same piece of music changes from a sound in the scene into score for the player. Not a visual crossfade (TRN-03), though the two often happen together.

## Use it for

Camps, safe houses and rest points; tavern performers and singers; car and handheld radios; story scenes where a character starts singing and the song becomes the scene's score.

## Tunable parameters

trigger (interact / sit / enter rest area / scripted) · transition time 2–4 s · start state: 3D attenuation, high-frequency roll-off, room reverb · stereo score version · ambience duck amount & low-pass cutoff · both layers kept sample-aligned · reverse on leave / stop / combat (authoring defaults)

## Request prompt

Add a diegetic-to-score transition to music sources in the scene. Play the same song on two synchronised layers: a 3D source on the object (distance attenuation, high-frequency roll-off, room reverb) and a non-positional stereo score version starting at zero volume. On the trigger (interact, sit, or enter the rest area), over 2–4 s fade the 3D layer out and the stereo layer in while reducing the reverb, and duck and low-pass the ambience. Keep both layers at the same playback position so the song never breaks. Reverse on leaving, stopping or combat. Accept: the song never restarts and the volume never jumps during the transition, and once it completes, turning the camera no longer moves the music left or right.

## Implementation hint

Play the same track on two voices started together: a positional emitter on the prop (attenuation, distance filtering, a send to the room reverb) and a non-positional stereo voice at zero gain. On trigger, drive one 0–1 parameter k over the transition: emitter gain and reverb send follow 1 − k and stereo gain follows k on equal-power curves, while the ambience bus ducks and low-passes with k. Never restart the track; if one voice has to start late, seek it to the other's playback position. Run k back to 0 on exit, stop or combat, or hand off to the combat music system.
